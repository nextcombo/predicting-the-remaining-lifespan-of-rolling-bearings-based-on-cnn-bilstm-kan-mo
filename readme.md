# Predicting the Remaining Lifespan of Rolling Bearings Based on CNN-BiLSTM-KAN Model

Preface
2.1 Introduction to the Product
The rolling bearing, as a key component of mechanical equipment, has significant implications for predicting its remaining useful life (RUL) in device health management and fault prevention. In response to the complex nonlinear dynamic characteristics of bearing vibration signals, this paper proposes a hybrid prediction model that integrates Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN), Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory Networks (BiLSTM), and K-Nearest Neighbors (KAN). To fully capture the multi-scale information of bearing signals, we selected 13 indicators with clear physical meanings and reflecting bearing status, including kurtosis, entropy, fractal dimension, waveform metrics, spectral metrics, frequency domain metrics, statistical features, and vibration characteristics. The CNN branch is responsible for automatically extracting multi-scale local spatial features, while the BiLSTM module delves into the temporal dependencies, and the KAN module enhances the model's understanding of complex degradation patterns through knowledge induction. The experiments are conducted on a typical bearing dataset from the intelligent manufacturing field, combined with the SHAP method for importance analysis of input features, validating the contribution of each indicator to the prediction. The results show that the proposed model not only improves the accuracy of RUL prediction but also possesses good generalization ability and interpretability, effectively assisting in bearing state monitoring and maintenance decision-making.

2.1 Dataset Introduction
This experiment utilizes the XJTU-SY rolling bearing accelerated life test dataset. As shown in the figure, the platform consists of an alternating current motor, a motor speed controller, a shaft, support bearings, a hydraulic loading system, and test bearings. It is capable of conducting accelerated degradation experiments on bearings under different operating conditions and obtaining complete data from operation to failure. The radial force is generated by the hydraulic loading system and applied to the housing of the tested bearing. The rotational speed is set by the AC induction motor's speed controller and maintained constant. The sampling frequency is set at 25.6 kHz, recording 32,768 data points per minute (i.e., the sampling time for each minute is 1.28 seconds).

3 Prediction Model Based on CNN-BiLSTM-KAN
3.1 Overview of the Model
The model mainly consists of three parts:
CNN module: employing multi-layer 1D convolution layers to extract spatial local features, with optimized kernel sizes and layer numbers through experiments. After convolution layers, batch normalization and ReLU activation are combined.
BiLSTM module: connected to the output of the CNN module, constructing a bidirectional LSTM layer based on the time dimension to capture forward and backward temporal dependencies.
KAN module: receiving the output of the BiLSTM and combining it with knowledge induction rules network, integrating prior knowledge with data features to enhance the model's ability to depict degradation trajectories. The final output is the remaining life prediction value.

3.5 Explanatory analysis based on SHAP
Using SHAP to explain the input features, the results show:
Time, peak vibration, and effective value vibration features are the top three important features affecting life prediction;
Spectral indicators also make a significant contribution to the model, verifying the importance of multi-scale information;
Statistical features have a slightly lower weight but play a supportive role at some special time points.
This analysis helps understand the decision logic of the model and achieves precise prevention. The explanatory analysis of SHAP enhances the transparency of the model and trustworthiness in industrial applications. In the future, further expansion of the knowledge induction mechanism and introduction of more cross-domain knowledge can be considered to improve the level of intelligence and industry adaptability of the model.
